# Referee Report A

**Manuscript:** *The Confounding Frontier: Exact Spectral Bias Laws, Calibrated Detection, and the Limits of Deconfounding under Dense Hidden Confounding*
**Referee role:** Random matrix theory skeptic (spiked covariance, BBP/BGN, Tracy–Widom, local laws, high-dimensional regression asymptotics)

---

## 1. Summary

The paper studies the linear latent-factor model $X=\Lambda f+u$, $Y=\beta'X+\gamma'f+\varepsilon$ in the proportional regime $c=p/n$, with four announced pillars: (i) an "exact capture law" giving the loading-conditional asymptotic bias of min-norm OLS along each population spike direction, coordinatewise capped by $\mathrm{cap}_j=(1+l_j)/(c+l_j)$ for $c>1$ and exact at $c\le 1$, with a ridge interpolation and a trimmed-$\tau$ law for treatment effects; (ii) a calibrated maximum-of-standardized-coordinates alarm (S2) whose BBP-based power frontier is claimed predictable to within a factor of about one; (iii) scoped impossibility results: universal blindness of eigenvalue-alarm statistics to sub-BBP confounding under response standardization, plus a closed-form visibility boundary for quadratic probes that is "born blind" exactly at $c=1$; (iv) a negative result showing EB-tuned soft spectral trimming collapses to OLS or loses to Onatski hard trimming, leaving trim-then-regress as the recommended recipe. The theory is validated by an unusually disciplined simulation program (hash-locked grids, pre-registered gates, adversarial falsifiers) and semi-synthetic benchmarks on three real covariate geometries, shipped as the `confounderalarm` package.

I verified a substantial fraction of the mathematics myself. Much of it is correct: Theorem 3.1(i) is a genuine finite-$n$ identity; the channel assembly $\mathrm{ch}_a+\mathrm{ch}_b\to\sqrt{l}/(c+l)$ closes algebraically in $t=1/(c-1)$ exactly as claimed; the BBP location $\mu(l)=(1+l)(l+c)/l$ and overlap $\xi(l,c)=(1-c/l^2)/(1+c/l)$ are transcribed correctly (and the authors' own erratum on an earlier wrong transcription is commendable); the null variance law $\mathrm{Var}(z_j)=(\sigma_\varepsilon^2+d_j/c)/\sigma_y^2$ checks out under A4a; the visibility satiation identity $\Delta(g)=-g^2(\omega_\star-\omega)/(v_0+g^2)$ is algebraically right; the $\Lambda_D$ arithmetic for the OLS treatment shrinkage reproduces. The reproducibility apparatus is exemplary. Nevertheless, my overall assessment is strongly negative: after rescoping forced by the authors' own falsifiers, the proved content shrinks to first-moment deterministic-equivalent arithmetic that is two lines away from known ridgeless-regression bias formulas; the manuscript contains **no complete proof of any theorem**; the flagship impossibility section ships zero theorems because its planned contiguity theorem failed pre-registered testing; and Proposition 3 (ridge capture) — one of the paper's advertised "exact" results — contains an internal sign contradiction and a scaling error in its own definitional display. This is a well-engineered simulation study with a thin theory core, dressed in theorem language the body does not honor.

---

## 2. Major comments

**M1. Novelty: this is BBP/BGN/double-descent arithmetic recomposed with causal vocabulary.**
Strip the causal framing and what remains is: the first moment of ridgeless least squares under a rank-$r$ spiked covariance with a dense mean shift in the response (§3), a maximum of standardized whitened cross-moment coordinates thresholded by Monte Carlo quantiles with a Gaussian-shift power calculation at BBP locations (§4), eigenvalue-overlap rates imported from Benaych-Georges–Nadakuditi (§5), and a comparison table of spectral estimators (§6). Every ingredient is cited by the authors themselves: Marchenko–Pastur, BBP, BGN overlaps, Onatski selection, Dobriban–Wager and Hastie et al. ridgeless risk/bias decompositions. The deterministic equivalent $E[\langle\hat\beta-\beta,q\rangle]$ for spiked designs is precisely the kind of resolvent-trace computation carried out in Hastie et al. (2022) for general $\Sigma_X$; specializing to Haar spikes and reading the result along spike directions does not create a new theorem class, and the authors' repeated boast that the proof uses "no local-law machinery" (§3, twice; abstract) cuts against them: it certifies that the result is elementary first-moment bookkeeping, not deep spectral analysis. The genuinely new-looking items — the closed form $\mathrm{cap}_j=(1+l_j)/(c+l_j)$, the odd-symmetry cancellation trick, the $d_j/c$ leakage tax, and the satiation law — are nice lemmas, not a theory. For a specialist audience the paper's contribution reduces to packaging. The arXiv keyword-search paragraph (§1.2, last paragraph) is not evidence of novelty; absence of keyword hits says nothing about the CCA and rectangular-spiked literatures that turn out (see M4) to contain exactly the mechanism that broke the paper's universal claim.

**M2. There is not a single complete proof in the manuscript.**
Every theorem-level object carries at most a "proof sketch": Theorem 3.1(ii)–(iv), Lemma 3.2, Theorem 3.4, Proposition 3 (ridge), Proposition 4 (ceiling), Proposition 5 (visibility), Lemma 6.1, Theorem 6.2. Worst cases:

- **Lemma 3.2 (off-diagonal vanishing)** — the load-bearing stone for the fixed-$r$ extension — is stated with **no proof whatsoever** ("All three rates follow from Haar frame moments ... exactly as in the $r=1$ ledger"). Joint-in-pairs control, uniformity over $j,k$, and the $O_p(n^{-1/2})$ claim are nontrivial and nowhere established. Consequently **the fixed-$r$ capture law (Theorem 3.4) is not proved**: its sketch delegates everything to an unproved lemma plus a Neumann expansion whose uniform invertibility of $M=I_r+A'K^{-1}A$ is asserted, not shown.
- No mode-of-convergence statements, no rates, no second-order terms anywhere. For a paper titled "Exact Spectral Bias Laws," delivering conditional expectations without error terms is thin. Distinctness of the spikes is required (ties excluded), growing $r$ excluded, and the CLT layer for $\hat\tau_{\mathrm{trim}}$ (§3.3) is announced with an unspecified variance matrix $\Omega$ and no proof, hedged behind a scope condition.
- The anecdote that an "independent reviewer" audited Theorem 3.1 (§3, proof-status paragraph) has no place as substitute for a printed proof; neither do the preserved "six naive routes" and bug diaries, which belong in a supplement.

**M3. Proposition 3 (Ridge capture) is internally inconsistent — a sign error with three visible symptoms — and its defining display is mis-scaled.**
This proposition is advertised in the abstract as part of the exact interpolation story, yet:

(a) The displayed chain
$$E[\langle\hat\beta_\lambda-\beta,q\rangle] \to \frac{-\langle\beta,q\rangle+\sqrt l\,\bar m(\lambda)\gamma}{1+(1+l)\bar m(\lambda)} = -(\mathrm{cap}(\lambda)-1)\langle\beta,q\rangle + \mathrm{cap}(\lambda)\tfrac{\sqrt l}{1+l}\gamma$$
is false as written. Substituting $\mathrm{cap}=(1+l)\bar m/(1+(1+l)\bar m)$, the right-hand side evaluates to $(+\langle\beta,q\rangle+\sqrt l\,\bar m\gamma)/(1+(1+l)\bar m)$: the sign of the artifact term is flipped relative to the left-hand side. Symptoms: (i) the claimed $\lambda\to0$ reduction "returns the min-norm capture law exactly" fails under the printed cap-form, yielding $+(c-1)/(c+l)\langle\beta,q\rangle$ instead of Theorem 3.1(ii)'s $-(c-1)/(c+l)\langle\beta,q\rangle$; (ii) the printed $\lambda\to\infty$ limit "$-\langle\beta,q\rangle$" contradicts the cap-form, which gives $+\langle\beta,q\rangle$. The left-hand expression and Theorem 3.1's convention are mutually consistent, so presumably $-(\mathrm{cap}-1)$ should read $(\mathrm{cap}-1)$ — but a one-sign typo that breaks both stated limits inside a proposition whose selling point is exact constants is disqualifying at a theory venue, all the more since **the proposition ships with no proof at all**, only a remark about why a previous (numerically rejected) ansatz was discarded.

(b) The definitional display $\bar m(\lambda)=\lim_n \frac1n\mathrm{tr}(G+n\lambda I_n)^{-1}$ cannot be right: eigenvalues of $G=XX'$ ($n\times n$) are $\Theta(n)$, so each term of the trace is $\Theta(1/n)$ and the displayed limit is $0$, contradicting the anchor $\bar m(0+)=1/(c-1)$. The intended object is plainly $\frac1n\mathrm{tr}\big((G/n+\lambda I_n)^{-1}\big)=\int dg(x)/(x+\lambda)$ with $g$ the law of $K/n$ — which is what the surrounding text and the (correct) quadratic $\lambda m^2+(\lambda+c-1)m-1=0$ actually use (I checked the quadratic against the Silverstein equation; it is right).

(c) The parenthetical "(so the fitted ridge estimator is $\hat\beta_\lambda=(S+\lambda I)^{-1}X'Y/n$ with $S=X'X$)" is incompatible with replacing $G^{-1}$ by $(G+n\lambda I_n)^{-1}$: the push-through identity gives $(S+n\lambda I_p)^{-1}X'Y = (S/n+\lambda I_p)^{-1}(X'Y/n)$, not the displayed mixture of unscaled $S$ with scaled $X'Y/n$.

Individually these are fixable; collectively, in the two propositions that carry the word "exact," they indicate the theory layer did not receive the same scrutiny as the simulation layer.

**M4. Section 5 — the advertised impossibility pillar — contains zero theorems, and its planned centerpiece was refuted by the authors' own falsifiers.**
The introduction states (item 3): "*We prove* that eigenvalue-alarm statistics are universally blind ... below the spike threshold, null and alternative are mutually contiguous, so no function of the eigenvalues alone, however augmented, separates them." Section 5 then reveals that the strong version — mutual contiguity of $\mathrm{spec}(M_{\mathrm{aug}})$ throughout the no-detachment region — "was fed to our own pre-registered falsifiers and did not survive," with fresh-draw AUCs of $\lambda_{\max}(M_{\mathrm{aug}})$ of $0.998$ and $1.000$ at $n=1600,3200$. One cannot "prove" in the introduction what §5 reports as falsified. What actually ships in §5 is: (a) Remark 5.1, an explicit data-processing triviality that the authors themselves flag as unable to "masquerade as a finding"; (b) a pointer to Proposition 4 (ceiling), which is near-tautological (tight coordinate means versus a fixed threshold imply power bounded below one); and (c) narrative. The OMH contiguity machine is invoked ("yields uniform asymptotics for the log likelihood ratio below a distance-to-threshold functional") but no theorem is stated, let alone proved, transferring OMH from the sphericity-plus-rank-one setting to the self-normalized augmented matrix whose first block scales through $\sigma_y(g)$ — a deformation the authors concede "has no analogue in the OMH alternative."

Worse, the falsifier outcome should have been anticipated from the literature the paper fails to engage: $\lambda_{\max}(M_{\mathrm{aug}})$ is, up to normalization, a canonical-correlation statistic between $X$ and $Y$, and detection of a low-rank signal through a response link undergoes its own phase transition at a threshold strictly below the pure-$X$ BBP point $\sqrt c$ — the rectangular/BGN singular-value framework and the high-dimensional CCA detection literature cover precisely this. The paper cites BGN-2012 for overlaps but never confronts the fact that the correct detectability threshold for the augmented class is not the pure-spike BBP threshold. "Below the BBP threshold ⇒ blind" is therefore framed around the wrong critical value for every statistic class except pure-X and the S2 coordinates.

**M5. "Universal blindness of eigenvalue alarms" survives only by statistic-class gerrymandering that the paper's own tables expose.**
After rescoping, the blind classes are: pure-X spectra (trivial), and the S2 spike-coordinate family (Proposition 4). Meanwhile the paper's own measurements show: partial-$F$ on PC scores retains power $0.262$ where S2 collapses to $0.022$ (§5.1 alignment stress test), and $\lambda_{\max}(M_{\mathrm{aug}})$ separates essentially perfectly ($\mathrm{AUC}\to1$). So in the exact regime where blindness is dangerous ($\theta=\pi/2$, subcritical profile), the manuscript demonstrates working detectors that lie outside the proven-blind classes and inside the would-be-augmented class whose impossibility failed. The honest reading — which §5 eventually makes — is that nothing of practical breadth remains of "universal blindness." Yet the abstract still headlines "eigenvalue-alarm statistics are blind to dense confounding below the BBP threshold" without the qualifier that the substantive class collapsed, and the package advertises a "blind-region certificate" whose theoretical foundation is now (i) a triviality and (ii) an unproved ceiling proposition about one statistic. Relatedly: if permutation-calibrated partial-$F$ dominates S2 at $\theta=\pi/2$ (uncalibrated partial-F already shows $0.25$–$0.93$ size inflation, i.e., signal sensitivity), the obvious competitor — calibrate the F-test by twin/permutation nulls — is never run. Shipping a detector that is beaten in the most dangerous regime by an uncalibrated version of a classical baseline undermines the "calibrated alarm" contribution.

**M6. The visibility boundary's "$c=1$ Marchenko–Pastur point" is a normalization artifact, and the proposition conflates asymptotic and operational blindness.**
Proposition 5 gives $\omega_\star=1/A$ (constant in $c$) and, for profiles $l_j\propto\sqrt c$, $\omega=w_0\sqrt c$. The celebrated statement that quadratic probes are "born blind exactly at the Marchenko–Pastur point $c=1$" holds iff $w_0=1/(2A^{...})$ — numerically, iff $w_0\sqrt c$ crosses $1/A$ at $c=1$, i.e., iff $w_0=1/2$ for $A=2$. That is a property of the authors' chosen grid calibration ("the scaling profiles deployed throughout the study"), not of the MP edge; choose any other proportionality and the crossing moves. Stating the coincidence as a structural fact about the MP boundary (abstract, §5.2 headline) is misleading. Moreover, asymptotically $\mathrm{sd}_0(q_0)$ vanishes, so the born-blind set is the codimension-one manifold $\{\omega=\omega_\star\}$: the asymptotic content of the proposition is nearly vacuous, while all operational blindness is finite-$n$ power budget — a distinction the proposition's "iff born blind" phrasing blurs. The AUC formula $\Phi(-|\Delta|/\sqrt2\,\mathrm{sd}_0)$ assumes equal Gaussians; the authors concede small-$g$ shape effects break it. And the proposition is unproven.

**M7. The Le Cam probe section documents that the invisibility program failed, and thereby weakens rather than supports the paper's thesis.**
Read as evidence, §5.3 says: a frozen 14-feature map fed to boosted trees detects subcritical dense confounding at $c=0.2$ almost immediately (AUC $0.845$ at $g=0.15$; $1.0$ eventually), via the single feature $\|b\|^2$ — i.e., the cheapest possible second-moment functional defeats the invisibility story at low aspect ratio, exactly matching the visibility law's deflation mechanism. What remains of "operational invisibility" is: one statistic family (S2) plus flexible probes at $c\ge0.8$ up to moderate $g$, at $n=2000$, in one simulator. That is an empirical operating envelope of particular statistics, not an impossibility structure. The honesty of the reporting is genuine and rare; but honesty about a collapsed claim does not convert the claim into a contribution. The abstract's phrase "claims are rescoped rather than asserted universally" is accurate and still oversells: after rescoping, item 3 of the contributions list is mostly a record of failure.

**M8. The estimand is engineered so that the headline effect exists.**
Definition 2.1 conditions the bias on the realized loading geometry $\Lambda$ (and $\beta$) and averages over the draw of $\beta$; the authors candidly note that redrawing $Q$ per replicate makes unconditional mean bias vanish by symmetry. Every headline number — the cap law, the "six-fold" OLS inflation, the invisible-yet-harmful region — is a property of this conditional functional. Conditioning on $Q$ is defensible (practitioners face a realized design), but then the detection laws of §4 integrate over redrawn $\beta$ (the leakage-tax variance requires $d_j\langle v_j,\beta\rangle^2\asymp d_j/p$ fluctuations across replicates), so the paper's bias statements and calibration statements are made under **different conventions for how $\beta$ enters the probability space**, and no section reconciles them. Which convention generated the overlay grids? If $\beta$ was fixed within cells for bias overlays but redrawn for null calibration, the two layers of the "validated pipeline" are validated under different models.

**M9. Simulation precision does not support the rhetoric of exactness.**
(i) The correctness gate tolerates 10% deviations with a give-up rule at 25% systematic deviation in 30% of cells (§3.5): generous gates for "exact laws." (ii) Coverage is non-monotone in $n$ ($91.67\%$ at $n=2000$ vs $100\%$ at $n=500$), explained post hoc by "profile composition" — an explanation that was testable within-tier and was not tested. (iii) The power-frontier headline ("median ratio 1.02", abstract and §4.3) is the median of **three** gated strata, one of which is off by 24.6% ($g_{\mathrm{pred}}=0.261$ vs $g_{80}=0.325$); quoting a median of $n=3$ is statistical decoration. (iv) On real geometry the frontier misses systematically: empirical 80%-power points sit near $1.4\times g^\star$ on every family (§7, PF-1), described as "well within a factor of 1.5" — the goalposts moved from factor-one (Phase 2) to factor-1.5 (benchmarks) without comment. Note also the benchmark statistic is **not** S2 but "S2-bench, amended ... after smoke testing exposed response-scale suppression and a bulk-shape mismatch" (§7.2), and its frontier comes from an "F12 slope law" that is never defined in the manuscript. Two different alarms and two different frontier laws appear across §§4 and 7 under one banner. (v) Benchmark negative controls realize size $0.000$–$0.003$ against nominal $0.05$: the procedure is severely conservative out of sample, which mechanically depresses the reported powers and invalidates any nominal-size interpretation of Table 1; the pre-registered band check literally failed on all six configurations and was adjudicated to GO post hoc. (vi) "Power 0.98–1.00 at twice the frontier": the lower endpoint is 0.975, rounded up.

**M10. The calibration claims establish "not rejected," not "calibrated."**
Across 31 null cells the share inside the $\pm2\sigma$ band is $96.8\%$ against a perfect-calibration expectation of $\approx95.4\%$ — indistinguishable, fine, but equally consistent with mild miscalibration given cell counts; the pooled $\chi^2$ p-value $0.41$ has power to detect only large distortions at 31 cells. The raw share inside the pre-declared absolute band $[0.035,0.065]$ is $80.65\%$, attributed to replication budgets (split-half SE $\approx0.014$, half-width $0.015$) — meaning the experiment cannot distinguish calibrated from materially miscalibrated at the declared band. The abstract's "size control verified over thousands of null replicates" overstates; "consistent with size control at current budgets" is what the data license. Additionally, the shipped analytic fallback (Bonferroni) realizes median size $0.1625$ — unusable — so outside simulation the package's validity rests entirely on matched-null MC quantiles, i.e., on the analyst being able to generate credible twins of their own design; under A5 violations (heteroskedasticity, acknowledged in §2 and §8) no guarantee survives. What, then, does the practitioner buying `confounderalarm` actually get beyond a carefully documented heuristic? The paper owes a direct answer.

**M11. The dominance "theorem" is shaped by hand-picked exclusions, and the abstract misstates its own oracle numbers.**
Theorem 6.2 operates "at deterministic-equivalent level" (i.e., not a theorem about estimators at finite $n$), over the class $\mathcal W_+$ from which sign-flipping maps and projection-type maps are "excluded deliberately." Exclusions of this kind decide the conclusion: projection-type maps are excluded because they transmit confounding at $O(\sqrt l)$ — but that is precisely a competing estimator family's behavior, and excluding it converts an empirical finding into a tautology. The corollary branded "the kill formalized" rests on Lemma 6.1, whose sketch (profile likelihood decreasing toward the corner $u=0$) is plausible but unproven, and whose scope (dense $\beta$, bounded coordinate law) conveniently coincides with the cells where the method loses. Finally, the abstract asserts a no-regret share of "4.1% even with oracle tuning"; §6 reports $4.12\%$ without oracle and $6.2\%$ with oracle. Even at the level of its own summary, the paper conflates its numbers.

## 3. Minor comments

1. Abstract: ~450 words, single paragraph, dense with percentages; the fourth contribution conflates 4.1%/6.2% (see M11). Rewrite for a reader, not an internal report.
2. Discussion cites "Cinelli and Hazlett" and "E-value approaches" with no bibliography entries; `schur2025decor`, `bai2002determining`, `chernozhukov2017high` appear in `references.bib` but are never cited in the text.
3. Matrix-transposition conventions drift: §3 uses $X\in\mathbb R^{n\times p}$ with $G=XX'$, while §2's verbal descriptions suggest column observations; state once, explicitly.
4. Definition 2.3 applies a white-noise Tracy–Widom threshold to designs known to be spiked, guaranteeing "outlier-positive" verdicts by construction; fine as defined, but say so there rather than discovering it in §7.
5. "OMH" is used before expansion; "Baing-type" (§6) appears to be a typo; "Marchenko-Pastur" hyphenation inconsistent with "Marchenko--Pastur".
6. §7 PF-1: "power ... in [0.98, 1.00]" includes 0.975; either widen or do not round favorably.
7. The "adversarially audited by an independent reviewer" and "six naive routes preserved as guardrails" passages (§3) are verification folklore; move to the supplement and print proofs here.
8. Proposition 4's constant $\kappa_j$ ("realized null scales") is undefined operationally — realized how, per replicate?
9. §3.3's trimmed-$\tau$ law introduces $\tilde\rho_j$, $\rho_j=1-\mathrm{cap}_{sc}(j)$, and $\mathrm{cap}_{sc}(j)\to l_j\xi(l_j,c)/\mu(l_j)$ with no derivation and no proof; the bounds $[0,l_j/(1+l_j)]$ are asserted. Either prove or label as heuristic.
10. Figure captions restate headline claims ("median ratio 1.02") rather than describe what is plotted; several figures are referenced but the underlying strata definitions (mixed profile, $\theta=\pi/6$) are scattered.
11. The novelty-by-keyword-search paragraph (§1.2) should be cut or moved to a cover letter; it invites exactly the counterexample search that M4 performs.
12. `references.bib`: `nava2026sdboost` and `ulmer2025sdforest` carry future-dated identifiers I cannot verify; ensure these resolve at publication time.

## 4. Recommendation

**Reject** (with encouragement to resubmit elsewhere after fundamental rework).

The empirical engineering, erratum culture, and reproducibility discipline are genuinely above the field's standard, and several identities are correct (I verified them). But judged as the theory paper it announces itself to be: the fixed-$r$ capture law rests on an unproved lemma; the ridge proposition is internally contradictory and unproven; the impossibility pillar collapsed under the authors' own falsifiers and ships no theorem; the surviving blindness claims exclude the very statistics the paper shows working; the "exact $c=1$" visibility boundary is a grid artifact; and the precision language (exact, verified, factor-one) is not matched by gates, tolerances, three-point medians, or 40% real-data frontier errors. What remains is a competent, honestly reported simulation-and-software contribution wrapped in theorem statements it cannot back. That is not a borderline theory paper; it is a different paper.

**Score: 38/100** (60 = borderline).

*Suggested path forward, for the authors' benefit:* print complete proofs of Lemma 3.2, Theorem 3.4, and a corrected Proposition 3; replace the §5 narrative with either a real contiguity theorem for a precisely specified augmented class or a candid reframing as a detectability phase-diagram study in the tradition of CCA/rectangular spiked models; decouple the bias and detection probability conventions for $\beta$; and rescope the abstract to what survived.
