# Referee Report B

**Manuscript:** *The Confounding Frontier: Exact Spectral Bias Laws, Calibrated Detection, and the Limits of Deconfounding under Dense Hidden Confounding*
**Referee:** B (causal inference / applied statistics, sensitivity analysis for unmeasured confounding)

---

## 1. Summary

The paper studies dense latent confounding in the proportional-regime factor model $X = \Lambda f + u$, $Y = \beta^\top X + \gamma^\top f + \varepsilon$, with $c = p/n$. It delivers four things: (i) an "exact capture law" giving the loading-conditional bias of minimum-norm OLS along each population spike direction, rescaled at $c>1$ by $\mathrm{cap}_j = (1+l_j)/(c+l_j)$, with ridge interpolation and a companion law for scalar treatment effects under trim-then-regress; (ii) a calibrated detection alarm (S2, max of standardized whitened cross-moment coordinates over selected spike coordinates) whose finite-sample null and predicted power frontier are validated on large simulation grids; (iii) scoped impossibility results: blindness of eigenvalue-based alarms below the BBP threshold, and an explicit visibility boundary for quadratic probes, with an adversarial ML probe audit forcing rescoping of the original universal claim; (iv) a characterized negative result: EB-tuned soft spectral trimming (the linear special case of SDBoost) collapses to OLS or loses to Onatski hard trimming in essentially all simulated harmful cells, so the surviving recipe is hard-trim-then-regress, packaged as `confounderalarm`. Semi-synthetic benchmarks inject known confounding into three real covariate geometries and compare against incumbent diagnostics. The verification culture is unusual and largely commendable: preregistered gates, hash-locked grids, adversarial falsifiers, honest rescoping, and a limitations section that discloses most of the weaknesses I would otherwise have had to discover myself. Unfortunately, transparency about weaknesses is not the same as repairing them, and several of the disclosed weaknesses go to the heart of the paper's practical value. My overall assessment: technically impressive machinery in service of a practitioner question that it ultimately answers only narrowly, with identification resting on an assumption whose failure mode the paper names but never measures, an impossibility narrative whose abstract-level formulation is contradicted by the paper's own Section 5, and a negative result aimed at a weakened version of the published method it indicts.

## 2. Major comments

### M1. The detection claims stand or fall with A4a, and the one experiment that would establish robustness to its violation is promised but never reported

Section 2.3 is admirably candid: testing $H_0\colon\gamma=0$ is meaningless without assumptions separating structural direction from spike directions, and A4a (delocalized $\beta$, $|\langle\beta,u_j\rangle| = O_p(p^{-1/2})$) is "the material assumption." The same paragraph then promises that "a dedicated alignment sweep quantifies tolerated violation." I searched Sections 4 through 7: no such sweep is reported anywhere in the manuscript. Every alignment sweep that does appear (Fig. 2, Section 7 sensitivity findings) varies the alignment $\theta$ of $\gamma$ (the *power* side), never the alignment of $\beta$ (the *size* side).

This omission is not cosmetic. Consider a fixed nonzero correlation $\rho_j = \langle\beta, v_j\rangle$ between the structural coefficient and a selected eigenvector. Under $H_0\colon\gamma=0$, the coordinate $z_j = \sqrt{n}\,v_j^\top b/\sqrt{d_j}$ carries a deterministic mean of order $\sqrt{n}\,\rho_j$: the statistic diverges and size tends to one. The isotropic "leakage tax" $d_j/c$ in the variance law (Section 4.1) covers only the *delocalized* leakage produced by Haar-drawn $\beta$; it does nothing against first-order alignment. In other words, in exactly the datasets where principal directions carry real signal (which is most of economics, finance, and genomics, and is the historical reason PC regression exists), the alarm will fire with high probability with no confounding whatsoever, and every rejection becomes uninterpretable as evidence of confounding. The paper knows this ("part of the signal mimics a spike and test size inflates") but never quantifies "how much alignment is tolerated," despite predeclaring exactly that experiment.

Worse, the calibration machinery cannot rescue this in practice. The matched-null twins and permutation controls used in Section 7 remove the beta-leakage component of the coordinate variances (this is stated in the gate mechanics discussion and again in Section 8). So the shipped null calibration is valid for a null (isotropic leakage) that fails precisely when A4a fails. All 39,200 replicate pairs of size evidence are generated inside the model's own $H_0$ (Haar $\beta$, Gaussian noise) or under permutations that strip the leakage channel. There is no semi-real null anywhere in the study in which $\beta$ correlates anisotropically with the design spectrum. As it stands, the paper demonstrates size control for a world that its own material assumption defines, and is silent about the size in the world the reader actually inhabits. This is the single most damaging gap in the paper.

### M2. What does rejection identify? The estimand problem, and the inflation of textbook material into "exact laws"

Section 2.3 correctly notes that only $\beta^* = \beta + \Sigma_X^{-1}\Lambda\gamma$ is identified from second moments. I agree, and I want the authors to confront the consequence more directly than the ledger does: for the vector target M1, the "bias" that the capture law quantifies is the deviation of an estimator from a parameter that no estimator, no test, and no amount of data can pin down. A practitioner cannot incur error relative to $\beta$ in any operational sense; the operational objects are $\beta^*$ (which OLS estimates perfectly consistently at $c\le 1$, bias-free by definition) and the scalar $\tau$ in M2 (identified by the structural equation itself).

Given that, the content of Theorem 3.1(i) at $c \le 1$ is the textbook omitted-variable plim identity $\mathbb{E}[\hat\beta] - \beta = \Sigma_X^{-1}\Lambda\gamma$, exact at every $n$, which the authors themselves concede is "an identity, unit-tested to $10^{-8}$." Presenting a two-line moment calculation as clause (i) of the anchor theorem invites the suspicion that the surrounding apparatus is decoration. The genuinely new content is the $c>1$ cap $(1+l_j)/(c+l_j)$ for the *minimum-norm interpolator*. But who runs minimum-norm OLS for causal estimation at $p>n$? The six-fold inflation headline (abstract; Section 3.3: error 0.064 to 0.391) is driven substantially by the denominator artifact $-\tau/(1+\Lambda_D)$, a pathology of deliberate interpolation that practitioners avoid with ridge, sparse regression, or any regularization at all (indeed the paper's own ridge interpolation exists precisely to describe that escape hatch, and ridge overlays sit at 0.0% median deviation). The practically meaningful residual content of Section 3 is then the trimmed-$\tau$ flatness result, which is a Frisch-Waugh-Lovell computation plus standard factor-projection reasoning, competently executed but far short of "exact spectral bias laws" as a contribution category.

### M3. The impossibility narrative: the abstract asserts what Section 5 retracts, and what survives is either trivial, narrow, or normalization numerology

This is where I find the manuscript's packaging genuinely misleading relative to its content.

(a) Introduction, contribution 3, states: "We prove that eigenvalue-alarm statistics are universally blind to sub-BBP dense confounding under response standardization: ... null and alternative are mutually contiguous, so no function of the eigenvalues alone, however augmented, separates them." The abstract makes the same claim ("eigenvalue-alarm statistics are blind to dense confounding under response standardization"). But Section 5.1 reports that the strong augmented-class claim "did not survive" pre-registered falsification, and that $\lambda_{\max}(M_{\mathrm{aug}})$, an eigenvalue functional of the augmented second-moment matrix, detects subcritical confounding with AUC 0.998 and 1.000 at $n=1600$ and $3200$, with separation growing in $n$. The sentence "no function of the eigenvalues alone, however augmented, separates them" is therefore refuted by the paper's own measurements unless "eigenvalue-alarm statistics" is silently restricted to exclude eigenvalues of $M_{\mathrm{aug}}$, a restriction the abstract and introduction never state. What actually ships, per Section 5.1's own layered accounting, is: (i) blindness of pure spectra, which Remark 5.1 correctly demotes to a data-processing identity "not a finding"; (ii) a power ceiling for the authors' own statistic S2 (Proposition 4.1); and (iii) falsity of the universal claim for the augmented class. An impossibility section whose universal leg is trivial, whose constructive leg is class-specific, and whose ambitious leg is false should not be advertised in the abstract with the verb "prove."

(b) Proposition 4.1, as stated, is weak to the point of vacuity: once sample-eigenvector overlaps with the coherent direction are $O_p(n^{-1/2})$, tight coordinate means give power bounded below one for free. The informative content (tightness rates) is cited from Benaych-Georges-Nadakuditi; the wrapper adds little.

(c) The visibility boundary's headline, that quadratic probes are "born blind exactly at the Marchenko-Pastur point $c=1$," is an artifact of profile normalization, not discovered structure. The algebra is in Section 5.2 itself: $\omega_\star = 1/A = 0.5$ (at $\sigma_\varepsilon^2=1$), and for scaling profiles $\omega = w_0\sqrt{c}$ with the fully subcritical choice $w_0 = 0.5$. The crossing sits at $c=1$ *because the authors chose the proportionality constant equal to $\omega_\star$*. Pick $w_0 = 0.25$ and the crossing moves to $c=4$; the mixed profile crosses at $c=0.044$. Presenting this coincidence in the abstract as the boundary sitting "exactly at the Marchenko-Pastur point" manufactures significance from a normalization choice.

(d) Finally, the "born blind iff $|\omega - \omega_\star| \le a\,\mathrm{sd}_0(q_0)$" criterion embeds a chosen AUC budget $a$; "born blind" language suggests absolute undetectability while the object is a budget-relative statement for a single carrier ($\|b\|^2$). Rename and rescope.

### M4. Value added over incumbents is thin, the head-to-head is against self-transcribed competitors, and the sensitivity-analysis literature is engaged without citation

Strip away the theory and ask what the paper tells a practitioner to *do*: upon alarm, perform Onatski hard trimming, a procedure available since Onatski (2010) and the Bai-Ng lineage (note: `bai2002determining` sits in the bibliography but is never cited in the text). The detection layer is the contribution, so it must be held to a high comparative standard, and here Section 7 (PF-4) disappoints. Table 2 labels the Janzing-Schoelkopf entry an "approximate transcription" and the UCM entry an "approximate proxy," both thresholded by permutation bootstraps implemented by the authors. The conclusion "incumbents lack valid thresholds off the calibration distribution" is then partly a conclusion about the authors' transcriptions, not about the incumbents' methods as designed and released. Rendsburg et al.'s consistency result in particular deserves a comparison under its own tuning, not a proxy.

The engagement with sensitivity analysis is weaker still. Section 9 invokes "omitted-variable sensitivity analyses in the style of Cinelli and Hazlett, and E-value approaches" with no citation (nothing in `references.bib`; Rosenbaum bounds not mentioned at all). These methods quantify, without Gaussianity, Haar loadings, homoskedasticity, independent rows, or A4a, how strong an unmeasured confounder must be to overturn a conclusion. Now combine that with the paper's own blindness map: eigenvalue alarms blind below the BBP threshold; probes born blind or operationally invisible through intermediate-to-high $c$ (chance level at $c=0.8$ up to $g\approx1.6$); power collapsing at concentrated link placement even in favorable geometries (0.05 versus 0.48 on A-main). The region in which the alarm delivers an informative, well-calibrated, alignment-robust verdict is the intersection of supercritical factors, spread link placement, and benign $\beta$ alignment, and the paper never attempts to quantify how small that region is under any plausible prior over $(l, c, g, \theta, \beta)$-space. Outside it, the paper's own workflow (Section 9) hands the problem back to sensitivity analysis. A skeptic will summarize the contribution as: a very expensive way to learn that in the easy regime you should trim PCs, and in every other regime you should do what Cinelli-Hazlett already told you.

Missing comparisons compound this. Guo-Ceviđ-Bühlmann's doubly debiased lasso is cited (correctly) as providing inference under hidden confounding, yet never appears as a baseline in the M2/PF-3 treatment block, where the comparison roster is OLS, ridge, and the authors' preferred trim. Factor-augmented and partialling-out approaches (the entire factor-model econometrics tradition: Stock-Watson diffusion indexes, Bai-Ng criteria, Giglio-Xiu three-pass omitted-factors method, none cited) are the natural incumbent family for "regress on data after removing factor-direction variation." Their absence from both the related-work section and the benchmarks is conspicuous in a paper claiming to map "the limits of deconfounding."

### M5. The semi-synthetic benchmarks cannot falsify the assumptions that matter, the frontier validation is loosely circular, and the treatment-effect evidence rests on one featurized 800-row subsample

Three objections. First, by construction the injections satisfy A1 (dense loadings), A3, A5, and place the confounder at spectrally chosen strengths; the real data enter only through the marginal geometry of $X$, which stresses the null calibration but never the loadings, independence, or homoskedasticity assumptions. A benchmark in which the ground-truth confounder is manufactured to satisfy every standing assumption cannot provide evidence that the method works when those assumptions fail, and those failures (sparse or discrete factor structure, correlated factors, heteroskedastic bulk edges) are the ordinary condition of observational data. Family A's dominant spectral structure is processing batch: an artifact known to exist, observable in metadata, and handled in practice by batch indicators, not a latent common cause of the kind the causal question concerns. Using batch-dominated geometry mostly re-demonstrates PF-2, which Remark 5.1 already concedes is trivial.

Second, the PF-1 "straddle" validates the predicted frontier at multiples of itself (power in [0.14, 0.21] at $0.5\times g^*$, in [0.98, 1.00] at $2\times$). Any monotone power curve with roughly the right slope passes such a test; the out-of-sample ratio near 1.4 (reported) and the alignment-dependent failure mode (Section 7, sensitivity) show the frontier layer is considerably softer than the Phase-2 "median ratio 1.02," which is computed over exactly **three** gated strata. Reporting a median of three numbers as a headline calibration statistic, with nine further strata assigned infinite predicted frontiers, is presentational overreach (see M8).

Third, all M2 treatment-effect evidence on real data comes from a single family: an 800-row seeded subsample of `k401ksubs`, random-Fourier-featurized to 1600 columns, with an injected factor ($\|\delta\|=0.3$, $\tau_{\text{true}}=1$). Calling this "real econometric geometry" stretches the term: the geometry that matters (treatment projection, factor linkage, joint covariance with the outcome) is injected or manufactured. The trusted-result reproductions (eligibility coefficient $+9.41$; IHDP adjusted estimate near the experimental benchmark) are plumbing checks, welcome but irrelevant to deconfounding performance. Section 8 admits there is no second applied domain; I regard this as disqualifying for the applied claims at present. There is no analysis of a real dataset of unknown confounding status in which the method changes a substantive conclusion that is later corroborated.

### M6. The "kill" of soft spectral trimming is narrower than advertised and is staged partly off the competitor's home turf

Lemma 6.1 is a genuine and useful finding about the *linear special case* of Nava et al.'s spectrally deconfounded boosting: under dense $\beta$ and $c>1$, the marginal-ML variance components drive all spectral weights to 1, so the tuned path reproduces OLS. But Nava et al. propose gradient boosting with an EB-tuned spectral loss and nonlinear base learners; only the linear member appears in the roster, and the abstract and introduction generalize the indictment to "EB-tuned soft spectral trimming" as such. If the collapse mechanism extends to the full nonlinear method, prove or argue it; if it does not, the headline is unsupported.

The evaluation grid compounds the scoping problem. All 97 "harmful cells" are dense-$\beta$ (A4a) cells, exactly the regime where the collapse lemma bites and where, by the paper's own scope guard, the variance components are unidentifiable. The 28 variant arms perturb errors, loadings, heteroskedasticity, factor correlation, $r$, and confounding sparsity, but never $\beta$ density. Under A4b-type perturbed sparsity, the authors concede the method "does useful work." So the kill is established in the one regime chosen to be lethal, and the regime where the indicted method was designed to operate (Cevid et al.'s perturbed sparsity) is excluded from the formal analysis: the dominance theorem's estimator class $\mathcal{W}_+$ explicitly removes projection-type spectral transformations on the grounds that they "are not estimators of $\beta$." That exclusion quietly evicts the actual published form of spectral deconfounding from the class in which dominance is then proved, leaving its default heuristic to be beaten empirically on dense-$\beta$ ground it never claimed. Within $\mathcal{W}_+$ on subcritical cells, Theorem 6.2 essentially formalizes the classical justification for PC regression: under A4a the signal is delocalized, so zeroing directions below the bulk edge destroys confounding transmission at $O(p^{-1/2})$ fidelity cost. True, cleanly booked, but folklore plus bookkeeping, not a surprising theorem; Corollary 6.3 ("the kill formalized") restates it.

I want to be fair: reporting that one's preregistered no-regret requirement (95%) was met at 4.12%, with oracle tuning moving it only to 6.2%, is exactly how negative results should be documented, and the byte-identity collapse evidence (31/97 cells) is convincing for the linear member. The objection is to the distance between what was killed and what the abstract buries.

### M7. Practical usability: the blind-region certificate is more likely to confuse analysts than to protect them

The package returns a "blind-region certificate stating when non-rejection is uninformative." Operationalize this for me. The certificate must be a function of plug-in estimates $(\hat r, \hat l_j, \hat c)$ and an assumed profile family (scaling profiles? which $w_0$?), since the underlying boundaries (BBP threshold, $\omega$ versus $\omega_\star$, alignment-dependent leakage taxes) live in parameter space that is itself only identifiable under A4a. The paper provides no propagation of rank-estimation uncertainty into the certificate, no statement of the certificate's own error rates, and no user-facing semantics beyond "non-rejection means nothing." Given the blindness map, the certificate will read "uninformative" in most of the parameter space a typical social scientist occupies (subcritical weak factors, moderate $c$), in which case the package's actionable output collapses to: run sensitivity analysis. Meanwhile the recommended adjustment (hard trim) is not free under the clean null (PF-3: 0.128 versus 0.110, a 16% error increase), the paper offers no decision rule for when trimming's variance cost dominates its bias reduction, and the timing of the full pipeline (matched-null Monte Carlo calibration per dataset) is never reported for the practitioner, only estimator-roster timings. Section 9's combined workflow is sensible as prose, but nothing in Sections 4 through 7 establishes that the alarm changes decisions relative to a workflow of "always inspect scree skeptically, trim leading PCs when $p/n$ is large, and report Cinelli-Hazlett robustness values," which is available now, assumption-light, and free.

### M8. Overclaiming and generality: the abstract repeatedly outruns the body, and the theory's scope is narrower than the title

Beyond items already raised (M2's "laws," M3's impossibility sentence, M5's frontier, M6's kill): the abstract's "median ratio 1.02" for the power frontier aggregates three gated strata; "inflates roughly six-fold" refers to simulator cells at $c=2$ versus $c=0.2$, while the real-geometry ratio is 2.43; "size control verified over thousands of null replicates" sits beside a raw in-band share of 80.65% whose standard error the authors themselves note is comparable to the band width. None of these individual framings is dishonest, and the body usually contains the hedge, but the cumulative abstract-level impression (exact laws, calibrated detection, mapped limits) exceeds what was delivered (one clean elementary proof at $r=1$, a Woodbury sketch at fixed $r$, a class-scoped ceiling, and a heavily instrumented simulation study). Everything proved is linear-Gaussian, Haar-isotropic, homoskedastic, independent-row, fixed-$r$; the universality workpackage was cut before proof (Section 8), so non-Gaussian protection is conjecture backed by panels; and "dense hidden confounding" in the title is load-bearing: the canonical econometric omitted-variable case, a confounder entering few covariates, violates A1 outright, and the paper's overlap machinery (by its own account, Section 2.3) becomes loading-dependent there. A reader of the abstract would not learn that the entire edifice presumes the confounder is already dense, Gaussian-flavored, and spectrally placed.

A recurring pattern also deserves flagging: predeclared gates fail and are narrated into acceptance. The control-size band $[0.02, 0.10]$ failed mechanically on all six configurations (realized 0.000 to 0.003) and a recorded REVIEW was "adjudicated to GO"; the frozen universal-invisibility declaration failed its own gate and was rescoped; the shrinking-with-$n$ correctness flag returned false and was reinterpreted post hoc (note D9); the frozen S1 implementation silently inverted its secular equation and was demoted before sweeps. Each individual handling is defensible, several are genuinely exemplary, and I credit the authors for documenting all of them. But a preregistration discipline that fails this often is not constraining the conclusions; it is decorating them. Future submissions from this program should either pass their gates or treat the failures as primary findings with the same prominence as the successes.

## 3. Minor comments

1. **Citations.** Cinelli-Hazlett and E-values are invoked in Section 9 with no bibliography entries; Rosenbaum bounds are not discussed at all in a paper positioning itself around unmeasured confounding. Add full references (with DOIs). Conversely, `bai2002determining` appears in `references.bib` but is never cited in the text; cite it where factor-number selection folklore is invoked or remove it. The novelty audit resting on the authors' own arXiv keyword searches (Section 1.1) is not a literature review; the factor-model econometrics and latent-factors-in-asset-pricing literatures (Stock-Watson; Giglio-Xiu omitted factors) should be addressed by name.

2. **Fig. 2 / alignment stress test.** At the blind angle $\theta=\pi/2$, the naive principal-component F baseline retains power 0.262 while the flagship calibrated alarm collapses to 0.022. The paper's own diagnostic is dominated by an uncalibrated incumbent exactly in the blind cell. The caption mentions this; the text should analyze it, since it bears directly on whether calibration (as opposed to raw power) is the right objective for practice.

3. **Unspecified convention.** Section 4.1: the nuisance pair follows "a shared frozen convention" that is never defined. The calibration formula $\widehat{\mathrm{var}}_j = (\hat\sigma_\varepsilon^2 + d_j/c)/\hat\sigma_y^2$ is unverifiable from the manuscript as written. State the estimators.

4. **Edge selector.** $k$ capped at ten is arbitrary and load-bearing for both size and power; report sensitivity to the cap.

5. **Rhetoric.** Corollary 6.3's title "The kill formalized" and the recurring noun "kill" are inappropriate for print; likewise "autopsy" (roadmap, Section 1). The science is serious; the vocabulary should be.

6. **Spurious precision.** Tracy-Widom statistics quoted as $48{,}430.8$ carry meaningless precision for a quantity whose null scale is $O(1)$ and whose value depends on normalization conventions that should be stated once.

7. **Abstract mechanics.** A single paragraph exceeding 400 words, containing undefined quantities ("no-regret share 4.1%", "$\mathrm{cap}_j$", "S2"), serves no reader. Split and prune; move numbers to the contributions list where they have context.

8. **Frontier semantics.** $g^*$ is defined at power 1/2 ($s_{\mathrm{detect}}$), so "power at $1\times$" of 0.48 to 0.55 in Table 1 is correct but will read as a defect to users expecting an operational alarm threshold. Define the semantics prominently, including in the package documentation.

9. **Proposition 4.1** should be folded into the text as a remark; as the section's only formally labeled result it inflates the architecture of Section 4.

10. **MMD probe.** Deferred for degenerate bandwidths, leaving "flexible probes" as a single boosted-tree family on a frozen 14-feature map. Either repair the bandwidth heuristic or stop referring to probe classes in the plural.

11. **Lemma 6.1 placement of scope.** The dense-signal condition under which collapse occurs is the entire practical content of the lemma; it currently lives in a post-proof "scope guard" paragraph. Surface it in the lemma statement and in every summary of the negative result.

12. **Terminology.** "Born blind" for a budget-relative, carrier-specific, profile-dependent criterion (Proposition 5.2) will be quoted out of context; prefer "blind at AUC budget $a$ for carrier $\|b\|^2$ under scaling profiles."

## 4. Recommendation

**Major revision** (reject-and-resubmit would also be defensible).

The manuscript contains real work: the $r=1$ capture proof is elementary and credible, the ridge reconciliation story is instructive, the collapse lemma is a publishable finding about the linear SDBoost member, and the reproduction standards exceed field norms. But in its current form the paper (i) leaves its detection guarantees hostage to an A4a-violation experiment it promised and did not run (M1), (ii) advertises an impossibility result its own Section 5 refutes in the only ambitious form (M3), (iii) demonstrates its benchmarks only under assumptions its injections build in (M5), and (iv) generalizes a scoped negative result into a headline (M6). Items (i) and (ii) require new experiments and rewritten claims, not prose; hence major revision rather than minor.

**Score: 46 / 100** (60 = borderline; the paper is below the bar primarily on identification robustness and claim-to-evidence distance, not on execution quality).
